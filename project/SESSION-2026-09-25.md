# Session summary, 2026-09-25/26: why the world model does not learn period, and what was changed

Written for continuing on DeltaAI. Everything below is in the working tree of
`/projects/bfrf/hibb/romae-lc` (18 files changed or added, not committed).
Run outputs are under `project/runs/`, job logs under `project/logs/`.

## 1. Where we started

The first stage-1 runs (`wm_w500`, `wm_w250`: window 500 / 250 d, 4 frames,
census ladder of 12 rungs, 50k steps) ended at
`val pred 0.034, probe acc 0.823, logP R2 0.356, shuffle 0.787, straightness -0.35`.
Reading those numbers:

- **Straightness** is the mean cosine between consecutive latent velocities
  (`cos(z_{t+1} - z_t, z_{t+2} - z_{t+1})`, paper Eq. 9). A fixed per-object
  point plus independent per-window noise gives exactly -0.5. Drifting from
  -0.05 to -0.35 means the latent collapsed toward a static per-object anchor.
- **Shuffle score** permutes magnitudes among the points of a window (cadence
  kept) and measures the cosine between the embeddings of the original and
  the shuffled window; 1 = blind to time. 0.79 is still mostly a bag of
  magnitudes.
- **Probe accuracy 0.82** was never better than the untrained encoder
  (0.84 at step 0 in the smoke run) and hides a 63 to 69% majority class.
- **Period R2** was carried by class-level period differences; within
  superclasses ROT was negative.

So prediction loss and surprise looked good for the wrong reason: the model
learned static per-object statistics, and "next window looks like this one"
became trivially true.

## 2. What we discussed and decided

- **Why period is hard.** A typical eclipsing binary or RR Lyrae has about
  700 cycles in a 250 d window and one ZTF point per five cycles; a rotation
  star about 57. Phase can only be recovered by folding across cycles, and a
  rotary rung folds a period only if it matches it to about 1 / (4 x cycles).
  Twelve rungs shared by every head and layer cannot do this; a window of a
  few periods holds only two or three points, so shrinking the window does
  not help either. (Result 4 below shows this argument was too pessimistic
  for the reconstruction objective.)
- **Lomb-Scargle period as an input** was rejected as circular for the goal
  of learning the period.
- **Overlapping frames** fix the dynamics collapse (give the predictor real
  dynamics), not period.
- **The decoder / reconstruction objective** is the one that demands
  period, phase and shape in the latent; next-latent prediction is satisfied
  by static statistics. The LeWM ablation against joint reconstruction is
  about pixel detail the predictor cannot forecast, which does not transfer.
- **RoPE budget**: half the head rotated by wavelength for two ZTF bands was
  wasteful; the rung count per layer is set by the width, not the head count
  (more heads only regroup the same rungs), so "bigger with more heads" means
  wider. nD-RoPE only matters with two or more non-time axes.
- **Evaluation** had to change: macro F1 and balanced accuracy, the majority
  baseline, within-superclass period R2 as the headline, a step-0 probe of
  the untrained encoder, and a hand-feature baseline probe.
- **"More patches"** means more frames per sequence and denser (overlapping)
  frames, not more tokens per window (tokens are fixed by cadence).

## 3. What was changed in the code, and why

### Package (`romae_lc`)

- `rope.py`: `AxialRope` accepts a per-head ladder (nested `[nhead][n]`
  list; `timescale` buffer becomes `[nhead, dim/2]`, angles carry a head
  axis). `ladder_rows` validates flat or nested ladders. `layout(...)` and
  `BlockRope` carry nested ladders through serialisation. `collapse_layout`
  folds per-head and per-layer ladders into one shared layout (sorted union,
  log-resampled to the block's angle count) for decoders with their own head
  count.
- `transformer.py`: `Transformer.forward` takes one `Rotation` or a sequence
  with one per block, so every layer can rotate by its own ladder.
- `model.py`: `RoMAEBase` takes `rope` as a layout or a per-layer list of
  layouts (`rope_layers`, `rope` = first layer, `rope_layout` for
  `hparams`, `rotations(positions)`); old checkpoints load unchanged. The MAE
  decoder side was factored into `decoder_layout`, `_mae_head`,
  `_mae_forward`, and `MaskedDecoder` is the decoder side as a standalone
  head over an encoder owned elsewhere (used for a reconstruction term next
  to the world-model loss). `RoMAEForPreTraining` keeps its attribute names.
- Why: to give the encoder hundreds of distinct timescales without more
  parameters, and to attach a reconstruction demand to any backbone.

### Project (`project/`)

- `common.py`: `Ladder` gained `layers`, `heads`, `deal` (old
  `ladder.json` files load as shared ladders); `RopeGeometry` /
  `rope_geometry(args)`; `measure_wavelengths` (n rungs from the
  periodograms), `deal_ladder` (`bands`: each head a contiguous band of its
  layer's sub-grid, layers offset by one grid step; `comb`: every head spans
  the range), `dense_ladder`, `check_ladder`, `get_ladder(args, records,
  out, geo)` with `--ladder-mode dense|shared` and `--ladder-deal`. Model
  presets `--size light|wide` (192x3x6 = 378 rungs, 384x6x6 = 756), `--mlp-
  ratio`, `--time-frac 0.875` (56 of 64 channels rotated by time),
  `resolve_model_args`, `encoder_config`, `rope_layouts`, `build_backbone`
  with explicit per-layer layouts, `flat_layout` for decoders.
  `fuse_frames` (factored from `fused_encode`), `FrameEncoder` (probe
  interface for a bare backbone), `batch_stats` (BatchNorm on batch
  statistics without touching running stats). Probes: `linear_probe` now
  returns macro F1, balanced accuracy, majority accuracy; `probe_metrics`
  adds `r2_within` (target = log period minus its superclass mean),
  per-superclass R2 with counts; `hand_features` / `baseline_probe`
  (shape, scatter, noise statistics after the per-band standardisation);
  `describe_probe`, `describe_by_class` (ROT first). `mae_state` /
  `load_mae` checkpoints. Frame defaults: 8 frames, advance (1.0, 1.5).
- `train_wm.py` (rewritten): dense ladder by default, curriculum on by
  default (`--advance-start 0.25 0.5`, `--no-advance-curriculum`),
  `--init-backbone mae.pt` (encoder, ladder and token spec come from the
  pretraining checkpoint), `--recon-weight` (+ `--recon-*`) for a joint
  masked reconstruction term, step-0 evaluation (`--no-eval-at-start`),
  hand-feature baseline line, `val_sigreg_bn_train` (SIGReg with the
  projector's BatchNorm on batch statistics), new probe metrics, resume
  rebuilds the backbone from the checkpoint's own hparams. The `baseline`
  log line carries `step`.
- `pretrain_mae.py` (new): masked pretraining (RoMAE recipe) on the same
  windows, ladder and token spec; every window of a sequence is a sample;
  resumable like `train_wm`; writes `mae.pt`.
- `bench.py`, `train_decoder.py`: adapted to the new ladder API
  (`flat_layout` for the query decoder).
- `jobs/train_wm.sh`: `WINDOW` (default 250) and `OUT` env vars, and a
  `PIPELINE=a.sh:b.sh` mechanism (each chain script pops the first stage
  when done; `THEN` is the one-stage form) because the QOS holds one running
  and one queued job, which one chain already uses. `jobs/pretrain_mae.sh`
  (new, chain; hands `train_wm.sh --init-backbone` its encoder, drops a
  leading `*.pt` argument). `jobs/train_wm_w60.sh` (new wrapper: window 60,
  log ladder 0.15 to 120 d).
- Tests: package tests for per-head ladders, per-layer rotations,
  `MaskedDecoder` equivalence with `RoMAEForPreTraining`, collapse; project
  tests for dealing, geometry, probes, `batch_stats`, and end-to-end
  pretrain-then-init and joint-reconstruction runs. 139 package + 21 project
  tests pass (75 of them also run on the aarch64 node).
- `README.md`: decisions of 2026-09-25, the two runs, the pipeline, log
  reading.

### Reading

1. **Masked pretraining learns period.** Within-class R2 0.53, ROT 0.58,
   RR 0.69, ECL 0.81, macro F1 0.50, shuffle 0.65 after 40 minutes. This
   exceeds what the folding-tolerance argument predicted for 57-cycle ROT
   windows, so that argument was too pessimistic: with a dense ladder and a
   reconstruction demand the encoder does resolve periods at 250 d.
2. **The world-model objective erases it.** Starting from the MAE encoder,
   stage 1 falls from within 0.50 to 0.24 and ROT from 0.42 to -0.21 while
   its prediction loss keeps improving and straightness goes to -0.36. The
   next-latent loss plus SIGReg, with gradients through both sides and no
   stop-gradient, actively removes period and phase because a static latent
   is its easiest solution.
3. **The dense ladder alone changes nothing under that objective**
   (`wm_w250_dense` ends where `wm_w250` ended), and neither does the
   window-60 run (it never leaves the baseline; straightness was briefly
   positive during the overlap phase, as predicted, then went negative).
   The positional resolution was not the binding constraint; the objective is.
4. The class probe stays near the hand-feature baseline in every stage-1 run
   and only the MAE beats it clearly (macro F1 0.50 vs 0.33).

## 5. What to do next

- Keep the reconstruction demand alive during stage 1: `--init-backbone
  project/runs/mae_w250/mae.pt --recon-weight 0.5` (or 1.0) is implemented
  and untested on real data; it is the natural next run.
- Or protect the encoder from the world-model loss: train the predictor on
  frozen MAE latents (a `--freeze-backbone` flag would be a few lines in
  `train_wm.py`), or give the encoder a much lower learning rate, or use a
  stop-gradient / EMA target for the next-latent loss.
- Do not spend more runs on ladder density or window size alone.
- The SIGReg running-statistics gap (0.8) is worth closing if the encoder
  is trained under SIGReg at all (evaluate on batch statistics, or replace
  BatchNorm in the projector).
- With the MAE encoder frozen, the downstream stages (decoder, residual,
  energies, injection) can already be run on `project/runs/mae_w250` latents
  once a `wm.pt`-style checkpoint wraps it.
